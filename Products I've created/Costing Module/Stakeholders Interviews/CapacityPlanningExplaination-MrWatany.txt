### **1. Meeting Objective: Financial Visibility & GPM**

**Antoon** initiated the discussion by defining his primary goal: achieving full visibility over the **Gross Profit Margin (GPM)**.

- **Requirement:** He needs to track every **Direct** and **Indirect** cost associated with every project and business line in the company.
- **The Logic:** Every item ("ftfuta") entering the company must have a known entry cost, and every item leaving must have a known exit cost to calculate the exact margin.
- **Current Status:** They have already established the cost structure for external workers (who pays for them and how they are allocated). The next step is deep-diving into Capacity Plans and Sheets.

---

### **2. The Capacity Planning Framework**

**Watany** explained that the foundation of their financial planning is the **"Capacity Plan"** (Planned Capacity), which forecasts resources based on client targets.

### **A. Revenue & Volume Forecasting**

- **Client Targets:** The plan starts with how many thousands of cartons/pallets a client is expected to bring.
- **Revenue Calculation:** These volume targets are multiplied by the price per carton to estimate **Revenues per Client**.
- **Breakdown:** The plan drills down to:
    - Daily output per client.
    - Storage needs (Pallet positions inside the warehouse).

### **B. Warehouse Resource Planning (The "Inputs")**

To handle the forecasted volume, Watany calculates the exact resources required inside the warehouse:

- **Manpower Structure:** The plan details the exact headcount needed, such as:
    - Warehouse Manager & Supervisors.
    - **Warehouse Men (25 workers example):** Broken down into specific roles like 5 for Receiving, 5 for Put-away, 9 for Picking, and 4 for Loading.
    - **Support:** Outsource workers required to support the core team.
    - **Housekeeping:** Cleaners required.
- **MHE (Material Handling Equipment):** The number of machines needed (Forklifts, Reach Trucks) and the drivers required to operate them.
- **Consumables:** Estimated tonnage of Shrink Wrap required.
- **Cost Translation:** All these inputs (Salaries + Operational Inputs) are converted into a total **Warehouse Cost**.

### **C. Logistics Planning (First & Last Mile)**

- **First Mile:** Calculating how many trucks are needed to bring goods into the warehouse (Inbound) and the cost per truck.
- **Last Mile (Delivery):** The cost of the delivery operation, which includes:
    - **Fleet:** Distribution trucks required.
    - **Personnel Hierarchy:** Delivery Manager → Team Leaders → Supervisors → Delivery Agents (DAs) → Night Drivers → Outsource helpers.

### **3. Business Models & Cost Allocation Challenges**

Watany distinguished between the different operational models and how costs are allocated in each.

### **A. CBC Model (e.g., Mostorod)**

- **Structure:** Contains both Warehousing and Delivery operations.
- **Costing:** Direct costs are easier to track because resources (labor, rent) are dedicated to this facility.

### **B. Distribution Model (e.g., Cairo, Alex, Delta)**

- **Concept:** Focuses on Sales Targets and Distribution.
- **The "Shared" Challenge:** In distribution centers (like Cairo), the warehouse space and resources are **Shared**.
    - Watany cannot easily split the warehouse labor cost per client because the same worker serves multiple clients.
    - **Solution:** Costs are estimated per carton or pallet based on a "Shared" percentage, rather than direct allocation of headcount.
    - **Revenue Logic:** Distribution is treated as an "internal client" for the warehouse. The Distribution arm "pays" the warehouse revenue for storage services.

### **C. 3PL Model (e.g., FedEx)**

- **Status:** Currently modeled for **Capacity** only (Current Capacity vs. Absence Rates).
- **Next Steps:** A "Cost Layer" and "Revenue Layer" will be added soon to finalize the profitability analysis.

### **4. Forecasting Methodology & Risk Analysis**

Watany detailed how they predict future volumes to set these capacity plans.

- **The Formula:**
    
    ```
    Last Year’s Figures+Seasonality Index+Increase Rate=ForecastLast Year’s Figures+Seasonality Index+Increase Rate=Forecast
    ```
    
- **Seasonality:** Taking into account peak months (e.g., March to September).
- **Volatility Examples:**
    - *Elano Water:* Forecasted to continue at 110k units, but dropped to 80k.
    - *Hayat Kimya:* Jumped unexpectedly from 50k to 300k units.
- **The Risk of "Planned" vs. "Actual":**
    - Resources (hiring) are based on the **Forecast (Target)**.
    - If Sales fails to meet the target, the operation is left with over-capacity (high cost, low revenue), leading to a loss.
    - **Mitigation:** Sales must inform Planning in advance if targets won't be met so hiring can be frozen to save costs.

### **5. Financial Analysis Sheets**

Two main sheets were discussed to track financial health:

1. **Profit Analysis (Plan):** Compares *Planned* Revenue vs. *Planned* Cost to show expected Cumulative Profit/Loss.
2. **Billing Sheet (Actuals):**
    - Linked to the "Lucas" system orders.
    - Tracks **Actual** cartons sold × Price per carton (e.g., 11 EGP/carton).
    - Updates daily to show the real "Gross Revenue."
    - **Antoon’s Note:** This sheet provides the "Selling Price" (Revenue), which is needed to calculate the Gross Margin when subtracted from the Direct Costs.
